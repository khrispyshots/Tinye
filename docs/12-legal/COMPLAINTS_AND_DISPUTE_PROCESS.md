# Complaints and dispute process — draft

**Status:** Draft | **Version:** 0.1 | **Last updated:** 2026-11-14  
**Responsible role:** Qualified counsel / privacy lead / product owner

## Purpose

Give users a clear, accessible route to challenge outcomes.

## Assumptions and dependencies

This is a documentation proposal, not an implemented or approved product. The master specification and owner approval govern scope. MVP means the proposed testnet release; later features require separate acceptance. Terms and shared states follow the [product requirements](../00-product/PRODUCT_REQUIREMENTS.md).

> **Unpublished legal draft. Not legal advice or evidence of compliance. Do not publish until counsel reviews the actual product, jurisdictions and every unresolved placeholder.**

## Proposed process

Submit a complaint through [SUPPORT CONTACT — TO BE CONFIRMED] with a safe operation/case reference and description. Never send wallet seed phrases or private keys. Tinye verifies identity proportionately, acknowledges receipt, assigns a case owner, investigates evidence and gives a reasoned response with an escalation route.

Financial complaints compare chain receipts, accepted amounts, reservation status and verified projection. An unresolved transaction remains unresolved; support cannot guarantee reversal or recovery. Reward complaints inspect rule version, source event and correction history. Privacy complaints route separately to [PRIVACY CONTACT — TO BE CONFIRMED].

## Timings and external escalation

Acknowledgement, response and escalation periods are [COMPLAINT TIMEFRAMES — TO BE CONFIRMED] based on applicable rules and operational capacity. Governing law/forum and competent external regulator are unselected. Counsel must preserve mandatory consumer rights and decide any mediation/arbitration wording; no binding waiver is invented.

## Acceptance

Accessible channels, case audit, confidentiality, conflict-of-interest separation and appeal review must be tested. Account closure does not extinguish complaints or justified evidence retention.
